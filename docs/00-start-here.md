## What "built-in AI" actually means on Windows 11

Your Windows 11 computer can include up to four different AI features, depending on
when you bought it and which version it runs:

1. **Copilot chat** — an assistant app in a side panel (or a full window, on newer
   versions) that you can ask questions, ask to draft things, and ask to help you
   change Windows settings. Think of it as a friendly helper that lives in your taskbar.
2. **AI tools inside the Photos app** — one-click ways to remove the background from
   a photo, erase a photobomber, or restyle a picture, right where your photos already live.
3. **AI tools inside the Paint app** — "Image Creator" (describe a picture, Paint draws it)
   and "Cocreator" (you sketch, the AI fills in as you draw).
4. **Recall** — available on newer "Copilot+ PCs" only. It takes a private, encrypted
   "snapshot" of your screen every few seconds so you can later search for something
   you saw but can't find ("that recipe with the chart, from Tuesday"). It is turned
   **off** until *you* turn it on — and this guide shows you how to keep it off, turn
   it on safely, or delete everything it saved.

A quick honesty note before we begin: features arrive gradually. Not every PC has
every feature, and Microsoft rearranges menus now and then. Where a button might be in
a slightly different spot on your computer, we say so — and every step in this guide
describes **what you'll see on your screen, in words**, so you can match it to your
own screen even if things have shifted around a bit.

## What you need before you start

- A **Windows 11** computer (Windows 10 doesn't have most of these features — we have
  a separate guide for that situation).
- About **five minutes** at a time. Every chapter is broken into short chunks — do one
  chunk, stretch, come back. No marathon needed.
- Your **Microsoft account** (the email you sign into Windows with) for some features
  like Copilot chat and Image Creator. Nothing in this guide asks for passwords,
  credit cards, or installs beyond what's already on your PC.
- Optional but nice: a real keyboard moment — every step here uses mouse clicks,
  taps, or keyboard shortcuts that are printed right on your keyboard. **This guide
  never asks you to edit the registry or run command-line commands.** We mean it.

## How to use this guide

Each chapter works the same way:

1. **A short, plain-English intro** — what the thing is and why you'd care.
2. **Step-by-step instructions** — numbered clicks, one action per step.
3. **"What you'll see on your screen"** — a short description in words of what should
   appear, so you can tell you're on the right track. (There are no screenshots in
   this edition on purpose; every step is fully described in text so it works with
   screen readers, printouts, and different Windows versions alike.)
4. **"Good to know"** — honest notes about privacy, cost, and things that might not
   be available on your exact computer.
5. **"If it doesn't look right"** — calm fixes for the most common hiccups. Chapter 8
   collects the big ones.

**Suggested reading order** if you're brand new: Chapters 1 and 2 (Copilot chat and
its settings), then 3 and 4 (Photos and Paint), then 5 through 7 (the privacy
settings — this is where every "which box do I uncheck?" answer lives, and Chapter 7
is a single printable checklist).

**Suggested order for privacy-first people:** jump straight to Chapters 5, 6, and 7,
set everything up exactly how *you* want it, then circle back to the fun chapters.
No wrong way to read this.

## Two quick conventions

- **Bold** means click that exact thing. For example: **Start** > type
  `Photos` > press **Enter**.
- Steps are written for a mouse, but nearly everything here also works with touch
  (tap instead of click) and with keyboard-only navigation (press the **Tab** key to
  move between things, **Enter** to activate, **Windows key + Ctrl + S** to open
  Windows Search hands-free).
