Chapter 1 · Copilot Chat: Your Built-In Assistant

Time: about 10 minutes · Difficulty: easy

If you remember the little "search" box in the taskbar, imagine it got promoted. That's roughly what Copilot is: an assistant that you can talk to in plain English — "help me write a get-well card," "explain the difference between a trademark and a copyright," "summarize this paragraph I pasted in" — and it answers in the same conversational voice.

Unlike the AI on your phone guide, this chapter is only about the advanced AI that came with your Windows 11 computer — the one that lives in your taskbar. Nothing to buy, nothing to install. Let's find it.

1.1 · Where Copilot lives

There are three front doors to Copilot. Any one of them works:

  1. The taskbar icon. Look at the right side of the taskbar (bottom edge of your screen), near the clock. You're looking for a small icon — often a tiny ribbon or swirl — that says Copilot when you hover your mouse over it. Click it.
  2. The Start menu. Click Start (the Windows logo in the taskbar), simply type Copilot, and press Enter.
  3. The keyboard. Press Windows key + C. On many keyboards — including any premium keyboard models shipped since 2024 — there is a dedicated Copilot key with the ribbon logo printed on it. Pressing it either opens Copilot or, on some PCs, opens Windows Search pointed at Copilot (you can change this behavior and we'll do exactly that in the next chapter).

What you'll see on your screen: a panel slides in from the right-hand side of your screen (or, on newer Windows versions, the standalone Copilot window opens in your app area — just a window like any other). There is a blinking cursor in a box near the bottom that says something like "Ask me anything…", a few blue suggestion chips underneath it (little clickable boxes: Write, Create, Learn…), and a small microphone icon that you can use to talk instead of type.

Good to know: If you see a sign-in screen instead, sign in with the same Microsoft account (usually your email address) that you use on this computer. Nothing extra to pay for Copilot's basic chat. A paid tier called Copilot Pro exists — you do not need it to follow this guide; when something requires the paid tier we say so explicitly.

1.2 · Your first five minutes with Copilot

Try these in order. Each one takes under a minute, and they're picked to show you the three most common "shapes" of Copilot: answering, drafting, and doing.

Step 1 — Ask a plain question. Click in the box where the blinking cursor is, then type part of a question and press Enter:

In one short paragraph, explain artificially intelligent agents like I've never heard of them.

What you'll see: Copilot "thinks" for a moment (you may see a moving shimmer or the words "Searching the web"), then a short paragraph appears, followed by small numbered links to sources on the web. Below the answer there are thumbs-up/down arrows and a copy button, plus a + New chat button in the corner.

Step 2 — Ask it to draft something. Type this and press Enter:

Write a short, warm paragraph thanking a nurse for a shift she covered with a smile.
Sign it "Dylan".

What you'll see: a paragraph arrives with a copy button near it, and often three buttons like Regenerate, Add more detail, and Shorter — those little buttons are how you "talk back to" a draft without retyping everything.

Click Copy, open any program (Notepad is fine), and press Ctrl + V — the draft lands wherever you want it. This copy button is the single most-used button in this entire chapter.

Step 3 — Let Copilot help with a Windows setting (the "doing" part). Type:

How do I change my screen brightness in Windows?

What you'll see: Copilot explains the steps — and on many PCs it also shows a small "Get help with settings" panel listing a clickable heading like Brightness. Clicking that can open Windows Settings directly on the right page. If you don't see that panel, no problem — the plain instructions still work.

Good to know: Steps like that clickable-settings trick depend on which Windows version you have. Newer versions of Copilot integrate more tightly with Windows; older ones just tell you where to click. Both are fine.

1.3 · Four everyday wins worth memorizing

Once the first chat went well, these four patterns cover most of what everyday users ever need. Copy the phrasing, swap in your details.

Win 1 — "Explain this." Paste any confusing text (a benefits letter, a lease clause, a car-repair estimate) and ask:

Explain what this means in plain English, and list anything that sounds like a hidden cost.

Then paste the text. Tip: paste the text after your instruction, on the same message, or you can paste an old message and hit Enter first — Copilot handles both.

Win 2 — "Make this nicer." Type your rough draft first, badly — that's fine:

Rewrite this email so it sounds less grumpy and more professional:
" need the invoice asap you should send soon. thanks."

Yes, that example is typed badly and grumpily — Copilot will still understand. Garbage in, polite email out. It's genuinely good at this.

Win 3 — "Compare these choices."

Compare buying an eBay-based vs an Amazon-based route for selling used books in a
table with three columns: effort, fees, best for. Short answer only.

Tables come back right in the chat, and you can copy them into any document — the copy button copies the table as text, and apps like Notepad get plain rows.

Win 4 — the Timeout pattern for slow days: ask for a outline before the full answer ("First give me a 3-line outline, then wait for me to say continue"). This keeps long answers from being a wall of text — a genuinely ADHD-friendly trick that we lean on throughout our other productivity guides.

1.4 · Side panel vs. full window

On heavily updated versions of Windows 11, the old narrow side panel has been retired in favor of a regular Copilot app window (sometimes launched via the taskbar icon, sometimes pinned in Start under "All apps"). They behave the same; the window just gives you more room and can be resized like any other window:

  • To make it bigger: grab its edge with the mouse and drag, or press Windows key + Up arrow to maximize it.
  • To get rid of it when you're done: click the X or press Esc in most layouts.
  • To summon it again anytime: Windows key + C (or your Copilot key).

Good to know: If pressing Windows + C or the Copilot key doesn't open Copilot, it may be configured to open Windows Search instead. We fix that in Chapter 2 section 2.4, where the toggle sits in Settings, not in the registry — no command-line gymnastics needed here, ever.

1.5 · The one rule of thumb with chat AI

Never paste anything you wouldn't stamp on a postcard. A lot of what you type gets processed in the cloud (we show you exactly where in Chapter 2, and which settings you can turn down). So the comfortable zone is: drafts, letters, questions, ideas, recipes, to-do lists, almost anything except Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, passwords, or other people's private information.

Everything else — go wild. Ask it to help write a chess study plan for a.m. mornings, draft a FFXIV free-company recruitment post, or explain DDR5 memory like you're on your lunch break. It won't judge, and neither will we.
