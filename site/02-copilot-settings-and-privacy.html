Chapter 2 · Copilot Settings & Privacy: Where Your Words Go

Time: about 10 minutes · Difficulty: medium (lots of toggles, zero registry)

Chapter 1 taught Copilot to talk. This chapter answers the questions a friend would actually ask over coffee: Where does my text go? Can I keep it on the device? Does Copilot have access to my other apps? Which boxes should I uncheck?

Big picture before the steps — Copilot has a few different "levels" of how much it reaches, and you get to choose:

  1. Pure chat. You type, an answer comes back. Your message is processed in the cloud unless your PC is a Copilot+ PC with the right model, and you can clear your history at any time.
  2. Web search. Copilot peppers in web results (little source links). You can avoid them by wording prompts like "from memory" — or leave them on; they're the most useful thing about it.
  3. Working with your apps and Windows settings. The deepest level: Copilot can open settings pages, and on Copilot+ PCs can be connected to things like phone calls or apps. This is where the genuinely meaningful settings live, and where a friend would say "you probably don't need to give it more than chat rights unless you get a real benefit."

Let's walk through every setting, one at a time, and this chapter closes with the "friend checklist" of what we'd uncheck.

2.1 · Open Copilot's settings

Step 1. Open Copilot any way you like — taskbar icon, Windows key + C, or Start > type Copilot > Enter.

Step 2. Look for your profile icon or initials — a small circle, usually in the lower-left corner of the panel or window (older versions: bottom right; it's a small target, take a second to find it). Click it.

Step 3. A small menu appears with your account name at the top and a list like Settings, Help & legal, Sign out. Click Settings.

What you'll see: the right panel (or the whole window) changes to a settings page with grouped items — General, History & privacy, and sometimes Language, Notifications, or Copilot voice. Each row is either a dropdown or a toggle pill that is on when the pill is filled blue, off when it's grey to the left.

Good to know: The exact rows vary by version. Work down whatever list you see; each one below is explained so you can match by description, not by exact name.

2.2 · The settings worth touching (row by row)

Go down the list in the settings page. Here is each row, what it does, and what we recommend:

Row: "Send messages when you press enter" / "Auto-send". The toggle that can annoy beginners: when on, Copilot sends the moment you press Enter before you finish reading over a longer message draft. If you find messages flying off half finished, look for wording like "Send on Enter", "Auto-send messages", or "Automatically send" and switch it off. With it off, you press a button that says Send — it won't go-by until you undeniably press Send.

What you'll see: after toggling, type in the chat box and press Enter on your keyboard: nothing sends. A Send button in the shape of a paper-plane or arrow at the right side of the chat box is now the only way to send. That's the "business-casual mode" for careful typists.

Row: "History" / "Show recent chats". Copilot keeps a list of your past conversations in the sidebar. Handy ("what did it tell me yesterday?"), but it's data about you sitting in the cloud. Options worth knowing:

  • Clear individual chats: back in the main chat view, hover over a past chat row in the left edge (or the little clock icon to open the history list), click the three-dots (…) menu next to it, and choose Delete.
  • Clear everything: in Settings > look for Delete all activity or Clear chat history and click it. Then confirm in the pop-up.

What you'll see: a confirmation box appears: "This will delete your recent chat history. This can't be undone." with two buttons Cancel / Delete. Pick your destiny.

Row: "Language". Pick yours if it guessed wrong. Some people like switching this to force simpler phrasing for English practice — it genuinely makes answers easier to read if you choose a "simple / plain" locale like English (United Kingdom) and prompt with "in plain English".

Row: "Mic / Voice". To be clear: Copilot's voice mode only activates when you click its microphone icon — it isn't listening all the time. That said, if you never plan to talk to your computer, you can skip the voice toggle entirely and also check the mic permission itself in Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone (Part of the full untick list in Chapter 7). Nothing breaks; the typing box works exactly as before.

2.3 · The Windows-level privacy settings Copilot relies on

Copilot also borrows a handful of permissions from Windows itself. These live in Windows Settings — the gear-icon app — under Privacy & security. Open it like this:

Step 1. Click Start > click the gear icon ("Settings"). (Or press Windows key + I — the letter I, not a number.)

Step 2. Click Privacy & security in the left edge. (Settings sometimes shows tabs at the top instead — either look is fine, find Privacy & security.)

Step 3. Take the list one item at a time, spacing over as many as exist:

  • General > "Show suggested content in settings app" — this is advertising in disguise. Toggle off.
  • General > "Let websites provide locally relevant content…" — means website language hints from Microsoft. Harmless, but off if you're minimizing.
  • Search permissions > "Cloud content search" — decide whether Start's search looks inside your OneDrive/Microsoft-account web space. If you want searches to stay on this PC only, toggle each cloud search row off. (Your Start results still work fine — this only affects cloud results.)
  • Tailored experiences > toggle off. This is Microsoft using account data to target Windows tips and offers at you. Less noise, no downside.
  • Speech — off, unless you use voice typing and want cloud-powered quality. (Voice typing still works offline in Windows 11, just slightly dumber.)

2.4 · The Copilot key: who it belongs to

If your keyboard has a physical Copilot key and you'd rather it opened Windows Search (the plain search box) instead:

Step 1. Go to Settings (Windows key + I) > Personalization > Copilot key — on some PCs this appears under Text input instead.

Step 2. A screen appears with radio buttons — tiny circles you pick one of: Copilot, or Windows Search (some PCs also offer a search-provider dropdown).

Step 3. Choose what you actually press that key for daily. Nothing breaks either way, and it's reversible with three clicks.

What you'll see: nothing at all — that's normal. The radio button just fills in. Status panels in Windows 11 are famously quiet. Try the key on your keyboard to see the new behavior immediately.

2.5 · The friend-checklist for Copilot

Copy this along if you want the "calm, private, still-useful" setup:

  • [x] In Copilot Settings → turn on nothing except what you use. Skip voice, skip plugins you can't explain.
  • [x] Set Send-on-Enter off if you've ever regretted a thrown message.
  • [x] Delete activity once a month — two clicks, and it's a nice fresh-slate moment.
  • [x] Windows Settings > Tailored experiences → off.
  • [x] Windows Settings > Search permissions → cloud content search off, unless you use OneDrive-located results from the Start box.
  • [x] The unbreakable rule stays: the chat is not a filing cabinet. Nothing past postcard-level privacy.

Everything on this list, plus the machine/Photos/Recall and OneDrive items from later chapters is consolidated in Chapter 7 · The Uncheck Checklist — one page to print or save.
