Chapter 4 · AI in the Paint App

Time: about 15 minutes · Difficulty: easy to medium

Remember Paint? The little program you doodled in, with the bucket fill and the airbrush? It quietly became one of the most surprising AI apps on Windows 11. The current Paint has:

  • Image Creator — you type a caption ("a tabby cat wearing reading glasses in a garden"), and Paint generates a picture from the caption.
  • Cocreator — you start drawing something rough, and while you draw, the AI fills in your style, in real time, as a duet with your sketch.
  • Remover — erase an object from an image you opened (same idea as Photos' eraser).
  • Layers and transparency — not AI but long overdue, and now real.

This chapter assumes you opened Paint (Start > type Paint > Enter). That's it.

Good to know before we start: of all the Windows AI features, Paint's AI is the one with the most variety between computers. Which tools you see depends on your Windows 11 version, whether your PC is a Copilot+ PC, and which region you're in. Everything below is arranged roughly as "most common to least common," and every section tells you what to do if the tool isn't there. Treat this chapter like a menu, not a prescription.

4.1 · Image Creator: describe a picture, get a picture

Step 1. Open Paint (Start > Paint > Enter).

Step 2. Look at the top-right corner for a blue button labeled Image Creator — sometimes it only shows a sparkle icon with the word beneath when the window is narrow. Click it.

Step 3. A panel appears on the right with a text box that says something like "Describe what you'd like to create." Also, the first time, a Sign in prompt — sign in with your Microsoft account, same one you use for Copilot.

Step 4. Type a description. Be specific and loose:

A warm cartoon of an orange tabby nurse cat wearing tiny blue scrubs, giving a thumbs up in a sunny clinic, flat illustration style

Step 5. Press Create (or Enter). Wait a few seconds while a set of images generates.

What you'll see: a small grid of several thumbnail pictures appears in the right panel — usually three or four variations of your idea. Each has a little download/plus button; clicking it copies the variant onto your canvas as a new layer you can then crop, edit, or combine.

Step 6. The image lands on the canvas. From here it's just Paint: crop it with the selection tool, add text with the A tool (a joke caption is basically law for cat pictures), save with File > Save.

Honest notes:

  • Image Creator looks the same across regions but this is a cloud AI feature — your caption and the results pass through Microsoft's servers. Captions should stay postcard-level private, same Copilot rule as Chapter 1.
  • Expect the occasional misunderstanding: it may generate a calm nurse cat when you asked for a chaotic one. Just click Create again.
  • There are content rules (no photorealistic public figures, no violence). When the tools refuse something, they say so — no mystery windows about it.

If there is no Image Creator button: your Windows/region version hasn't activated it yet for your account. The Microsoft Paint app updates from the Microsoft Store (Store app > search "Paint" > Update if offered). Even after updating, some regions get it later than others — no registry hacking required or possible, honestly; often you just wait a bit and check the Store again.

4.2 · Cocreator: draw badly, finish beautifully

This is the tool that surprises everyone most: it requires a Copilot+ PC (recent laptops with a Neural Processing Unit). If you have one, this is worth trying even if you think you can't draw — especially if you think you can't draw.

Step 1. In Paint, look for the Cocreator button in the toolbar (a sparkle-and-brush icon, near Image Creator).

Step 2. The first time, accept the on-device model download prompt (a notification-style dialog mentioning the software being downloaded to your PC — it's a one-time ~hundred-megabytes process on Wi-Fi) and agree to the rules of use.

Step 3. In the panel on the right set Creativity using a slider from Low to High. Two-way rule of thumb: the more you actually draw, the lower you dial this; the more you want the AI to take over, the higher.

Step 4. Type a short description of what you're drawing ("a lighthouse on a foggy cliff at dusk"), then pick a color and just… draw shapes with the pencil. As you sketch, the canvas mostly fills in in real time, keeping your strokes and adding theirs.

What you'll see: the canvas evolves continuously while you draw: lines you make stay, and the AI repaints the rest in a style you can nudge. It feels less like a tool and more like drawing next to a friend who is eerily good.

Step 5. When you like a version, click Apply or Stop to freeze it, and save. Undo works on AI fills exactly like everything else.

Honest notes: on low creativity, results stick close to your sketch (good for coloring book pages); on high, it may go somewhere unexpected — squash and draw again. And the whole file stays on your machine: Cocreator runs on the device once downloaded, which is a genuinely nice contrast with image-creator-style services.

4.3 · Remover (erase an object in Paint)

Step 1. Open any photo in Paint (File > Open, or right-click a photo in File Explorer > Open with > Paint).

Step 2. Find the Removal or Remover tool in the Paint toolbar (eraser with a sparkle).

Step 3. Brush over the thing to remove (a sign, a trash bag, a person on a hiking track), release, wait a beat.

What you'll see: a light mask over the brushed area, the area then re-fills and cleans up. The undo arrow in the quick access bar (top area) reverses it — explore a few tries; each try is a fresh result.

Step 4. Click Save when you're happy. As always in this book — save as a new copy; your original is precious.

4.4 · A full picture of Paint's AI and privacy

Quick recap to decide what's comfortable to you:

  • Image Creator → runs in cloud. Your description and generated image travel through Microsoft's servers. (Low-risk if your descriptions are ordinary.)
  • Cocreator → downloaded model, runs on your PC. Your sketch stays with you.
  • Remover → runs on-device in current versions on Copilot+ machines; on other PCs, may fall back to the cloud and follow the same rules as Image Creator.
  • Save behavior → everything saves to your own Pictures folder. Paint isn't auto-signing your files to OneDrive or anything sneaky.

When you're done making cat scrubs illustrations, close Paint like always. Next chapter is a big one: Recall, the privacy decisions that come with it, and exactly which switches you should think hard about before flipping.
